# Institutional Research Method — seven pillars

## Stance
This is source-based fundamental analysis, not brokerage recommendation and not an exhaustive paraphrase. Research begins with the key economic question and ends with **potential falsification**. Separate:
- D: disclosed and directly attributable fact
- M: management's explanation, position or forecast (not independently verified)
- C: calculation reproducible from sourced data
- I: analyst's conditional inference
- U: unresolved/unknown; no number should be made up

## Analyst workflow and memo
1. 120-word company model: product/service, customers, value chain, billing/revenue recognition, supply chain and cost/value driver. Cite specific pages.
2. Write 3 competing candidate theses; grade importance using size of financial delta, persistence, decision relevance, evidence sufficiency and disconfirmability (each 0–2). Pick top thesis, note runner-up.
3. Create "change log": revenue, GP, expenses, parent and recurring profit, CFO, FCF, AR, inventory, CapEx, finance income/FX, customer concentration, R&D, payout. Every material line has current, comparable, delta, % with denominator and temporal basis.
4. Ask **what changed / what caused it / what else might explain it / why it matters / what would prove us wrong** for every major visual.
5. Business quality: pricing power vs input relief vs product mix, recurring versus nonrecurring earnings, client dependence, technology displacement, unit economics if disclosed, incremental return on invested capital if comparable.
6. Balance + cash: cash conversion trend, inventory raw vs WIP vs finished, AR aging/allowance, cash equivalent vs total monetary funds, payable financing, restricted deposits, maturity, financial assets, development capitalization, fixed assets and CIP.
7. Capital: announced projects vs cash CapEx vs CIP/PP&E; equity issuance, employee incentive dilutions and share-split adjusted EPS; profit distribution, overseas investment, dividend policy. Avoid claiming management capital-allocation IRR unless provided.
8. Risks: supplier concentration, customer concentration, geopolitics/FX, technology and pricing substitution, customer capex cycles, operational delivery, audit/accounting discretion, governance/pledge/related-party.
9. Competing explanations and contradictions: give the same analytical effort to the alternative thesis. Good research is the smallest set of reliable statements that survives scrutiny.
10. Build exact "monitor next" watchlist: variable, current observed value and PDF page, relevant follow-up disclosure, directional interpretation; no invented target price or forecast.

## Driver bridge decision tree
- Revenue = Σ(price × quantity) ONLY if comparable quantities, prices and product taxonomy disclosed.
- Where no clean price/volume data, use segment sales changes and **label residual unknown**. Pervasive rounding in printed growth rates makes reconstructed prior segment amounts APPROXIMATE, not original facts.
- GP change can be decomposed: ΔR × initial margin + current R × margin change (single product) as a valid mathematical bridge; for multi-product use consistent symmetric/Shapley decomposition on covered products, then separately residual, and distinguish within-product vs mix. A formula is not the same as evidence of business mechanism.
- Profit quality: reported net profit ↔ adjusted parent nonrecurring, net profit/CFO bridges, R&D, government aid, FX and fair value; major one-offs should be identified, not casually removed.
- Free cash flow: explicitly annotate the analyst definition and capex that may include intangible and non-operating asset purchases; noncash investments and financing excluded. Cash interest/lease treatment depends on accounting and availability.
- Capital returns: don't calculate ROIC from one point-in-time equity or auto annualize a half-year. If required inputs are absent, state NOT_ESTABLISHED.

## Research note page contract
Research header: company, security code, period, issuer report version, unaudited/audited, original PDF SHA.
Opening: primary thesis (20–30 words), 2 strongest supports, 1 opposing fact and why it matters.
Body: 4–6 segments chosen by *decision value*, not a table-of-contents mirror.
Each segment: claim → evidence with p. → calculation → alternative → assessment → indicator.
End: unresolved important questions, evidence calendar, audit/limitations, data glossary, sources.
Source PDF-only cannot conclusively establish external competitive ranking, sector TAM, independent price forecasts, trading multiples or future shipment outcomes.

## Cross-source verification when expanded mode is authorized
Independent hierarchy: issuer audited report and exchange filings; regulator; validated peer statements; vetted industry research and primary customer/supplier filings; management earnings calls; secondary commentary. Cite as-of and scope. Check revisions before comparisons. Exchange announcements are not automatically analyst-confirmed operating outcomes.
